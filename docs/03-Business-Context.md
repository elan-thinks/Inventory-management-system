# 3. Business Context

## 3.1 Type of Business

**NovaTech Electronics** is a small-to-medium independent retailer specialising in consumer electronics, computer accessories, mobile phone accessories, and related gadgets.

Typical product categories include:

- Headphones & Audio
- Phone Cases & Chargers
- Computer Peripherals (mice, keyboards, webcams)
- Storage Devices (USB drives, external HDDs)
- Cables & Adapters
- Smart Home Accessories

The business operates from a single physical store with an attached small stock room. It purchases products from a limited number of trusted suppliers and sells primarily to individual customers and a few small local businesses.

## 3.2 What Inventory It Manages

Physical products that:

- Have a unique product identity
- Belong to a category
- Are supplied by one or more suppliers
- Have purchase price and selling price
- Have a current quantity on hand
- Have a defined minimum (reorder) stock level

## 3.3 Why Inventory Management Is Necessary

- Prevent stock-outs that cause lost sales and customer dissatisfaction
- Avoid overstocking that locks capital in slow-moving items
- Maintain accurate cost and selling price information
- Provide a reliable history of all stock movements for accountability
- Enable the owner to make informed purchasing decisions
- Reduce time spent on manual stock counts and spreadsheet updates

## 3.4 Who Uses the System

| Role                  | Typical Person                          | Frequency of Use      |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Administrator         | Store Owner / Manager                   | Daily / as needed     |
| Inventory / Store Staff | Sales assistants / stock clerks       | Multiple times per day|

## 3.5 Problems with Manual Inventory Management

- Spreadsheets become outdated quickly
- Multiple people editing the same file causes conflicts
- No automatic enforcement of business rules (e.g., cannot sell more than available stock)
- Difficult to answer “How many of product X do we have right now?”
- Hard to identify which products need reordering
- No easy way to see recent stock movements
- Risk of data loss or accidental deletion
- Time-consuming to produce even basic reports

## 3.6 How the Proposed System Addresses These Problems

- Centralised, consistent data store
- Enforced business rules and validation
- Real-time (or near real-time) stock quantity updates on every stock-in / stock-out
- Clear low-stock and out-of-stock indicators
- Fast search and filtering
- Automatic recording of who performed each transaction and when
- Simple, printable reports
- Role-based access so only authorised users can perform sensitive operations
